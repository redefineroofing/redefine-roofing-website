Roof Repair in St. Petersburg — Where the Leaks Actually Start

St. Pete's mid-century block homes leak at the low-slope transitions, the bungalows leak at aging flashing, and Shore Acres leaks after the storm. We fix the right thing.

The Low-Slope Leak Most St. Pete Roofers Miss

St. Petersburg's signature 1950s and 60s concrete-block homes almost all share one weak point: a low-slope or flat rear addition tied into the main shingle roof. The seam where those two pitches meet is where these homes leak, and it's the spot a shingle-only crew tends to botch by running shingles past their rated slope. We repair those transitions with proper low-slope membrane materials integrated into the shingle field, so the water actually goes where it should.

Bungalow Districts: Old Roofs, Careful Repairs

In Historic Kenwood and the Old Northeast, the bungalows carry some of the oldest roofs in the county — plank decking, aged flashing, and character worth protecting. Most of our repairs here are component-level: pipe boots cracked by Florida UV, flashing pulling away at chimneys and dormers, worn valleys. We match the existing shingles as closely as the weathered roof allows and back the work with our lifetime workmanship warranty.

Shore Acres Knows Storms — So Do We

Neighborhoods like Shore Acres and Snell Isle live with water and wind in a way the rest of Pinellas only reads about. After a storm crosses St. Pete, wind-lifted shingles and broken sealant bonds turn into leaks — often weeks later. Our storm team documents and repairs the damage, with same-day tarping when needed. Repairs permit through the City of St. Petersburg, handled by us. Licensed and insured, CCC 1331457.

Common St. Petersburg Roof Repairs We Handle

The repairs we make most across St. Pete reflect its housing mix:

  • Flat-to-shingle transition leaks on 1950s-60s block-home additions
  • Failed pipe boots and flashing on Kenwood and Old Northeast bungalows
  • Wind-lifted shingles after storms cross Shore Acres and the coast
  • Low-slope membrane repairs on flat rear sections
  • Aged valley and drip-edge replacement on older roofs

St. Petersburg Roof Repair: Questions We Hear

My St. Pete home has a flat section that keeps leaking. Can you fix it?

Yes — this is one of the most common repairs we do in St. Petersburg. The flat or low-slope section usually needs a proper membrane repair where it ties into the shingle roof, not more shingles. We fix the transition correctly so it stops recurring.

Do you repair roofs on the older bungalows in Kenwood and Old Northeast?

Regularly. Pre-war bungalows leak mostly at flashing, boots, and valleys rather than the shingle field. We repair those components, match materials to the district's character, and handle the older decking underneath honestly.

How soon should I get a repair after a storm hits Shore Acres?

Sooner is better. Wind damage is often invisible from the ground and Florida's claim-reporting window is limited, so a documented inspection right after a storm protects both your roof and your claim. We respond 24/7 with same-day tarping when conditions allow.

Is a small roof repair worth it, or am I just delaying a replacement?

Often it's genuinely worth it — a failed boot or flashing on a sound roof is a real fix, not a band-aid. We'll tell you plainly which situation you're in, with photos, so you're not spending on a patch when the roof is actually done.
