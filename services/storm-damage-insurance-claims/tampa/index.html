Tampa's Storms and the First 48 Hours

Tampa takes the brunt of summer thunderstorm wind, tropical-system gusts, and the wind-driven rain that finds every weak flashing on a Seminole Heights bungalow or New Tampa two-story. The first priority after a storm is stopping further damage — our crews provide 24/7 emergency tarping so a compromised roof doesn't turn one storm into weeks of interior damage. Then we inspect thoroughly and document everything with photos before a single repair, because that record is what a claim stands on.

Your Claim Ally, Not the Carrier's

We work the insurance process for the homeowner. We document the damage the way an adjuster needs to see it, meet the adjuster on-site, and make sure legitimate storm damage isn't written off as "wear and tear." Inside city limits your permit runs through the City of Tampa, and we pull it. Florida now sets tight deadlines to file a windstorm or hurricane claim — generally within a year of the date of loss — so the worst move is to wait.

What to Watch For After a Tampa Storm

  • Shingles lifted, creased, or scattered in the yard
  • Granules washed into gutters and downspout splash zones
  • Dented soft metals — vents, flashing, gutters — a sign of hail
  • New interior stains on ceilings or around skylights
  • Displaced ridge caps or exposed nail heads

Not every storm does claimable damage, and we'll tell you honestly if yours didn't. Licensed and insured, CCC 1331457.

Tampa Storm Damage & Claims: Common Questions

Do you offer emergency roof tarping in Tampa after a storm?

Yes — 24/7. When a storm opens your roof, fast temporary protection prevents far more expensive interior damage. We tarp to stop the bleeding, then schedule a full inspection and the permanent repair.

Will you help with my Tampa homeowners insurance claim?

Yes, and we work the process for you. We document the storm damage the way an adjuster needs to see it, meet the adjuster on-site, and advocate that legitimate damage is covered — not dismissed as normal wear.

How long do I have to file a storm claim in Florida?

Florida law now sets tight deadlines — generally within one year of the date of loss for a windstorm or hurricane claim, with a shorter window for supplemental claims. Check your specific policy, but the practical answer is: don't wait. Get it documented and filed promptly.

Someone offered to waive my deductible if I sign with them. Is that legit?

No — waiving or 'covering' a homeowner's insurance deductible is illegal in Florida and a red flag for fraud. A reputable contractor documents real damage and collects the deductible the law requires. Anyone promising otherwise should be avoided.

What if the storm didn't actually damage my roof?

Then we'll tell you, in writing, and you'll have a documented inspection for your records at no obligation. We'd rather keep your trust than push a claim that isn't there.
